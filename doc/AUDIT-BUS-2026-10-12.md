## Findings

### 1. P1 — BUSCR locking can permanently block instruction fetch

The [BIU requester arbiter](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_biu.sv:196) grants only `lock_c` while `locked` is set. A BUSCR-locked operand belongs to DMU client 0; instruction fetch is client 1. If executing the locked sequence requires another instruction transfer, the fetch cannot obtain the bus, and the processor cannot reach the operand write that would end the lock.

The [full-CPU probe](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/buscr_fetch_stall.s) disables caching, sets BUSCR to `$A0000000`, reads `$9000`, executes register-only MOVEQ instructions, and then writes `$9000`. It times out at PC `$422` at full BCLK and `$41E` with half BCLK, waits, and burst inhibition. At cycle 2000 both runs show `locked=1`, `req=10`, `grants=00`: instruction fetch is pending, and the DMU has no request. See the [full-clock log](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/buscr_fetch_stall-full.log) and [half-clock log](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/buscr_fetch_stall-half.log).

Both controls pass at both speeds: [BUSCR left clear](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/buscr_unlocked_control.s), and [the locked sequence first warmed into the instruction cache](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/buscr_cached_control.s).

MC68060UM §7.4, pp. 7-4–7-5, provides BUSCR for CAS2 emulation and critical code sequences. Locking starts with the next operand access and ends with the LOCKE write or architectural termination; instruction fetching must remain possible. WinUAE's [MOVEC implementation](/home/adam/WinUAE/newcpu_common.cpp:156) records BUSCR without imposing a requester lock on instruction fetch.

Allow necessary processor instruction transfers while preserving the external locked tenure. Changing requester selection alone is insufficient: [transfer startup](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_biu.sv:644) also derives LOCK directly from the newly selected request, which would negate it on an ordinary fetch.

### 2. P2 — NOP prematurely releases an active BUSCR lock

The BIU's [unlock input](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060.sv:177) is connected to `kill_now`, whose [definition](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_backend.sv:1351) includes ordinary pipeline redirects. NOP is serialized by this core and retires with a redirect. The [BIU unlock handling](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_biu.sv:377) consequently clears the external lock even though BUSCR.L and BUSCR.LE remain set and the final operand write has not occurred.

The [NOP probe](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/buscr_nop_lock.s) opens the lock with a read from `$9000`, executes NOPs, then performs the final write. At full speed the read completes at cycle 534 with LOCK asserted; LOCK negates at cycle 544, while L=1 and LE=1. The [pin observer fails](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/buscr_nop_lock-full.log), although the program eventually reports architectural PASS. The half-clock/waited variant also fails.

MC68060UM §7.4, p. 7-5, requires continuity until the final acknowledged LOCKE write, software clearing, or exception. NOP performs none of these. WinUAE's [NOP implementation](/home/adam/WinUAE/cpuemu_40.cpp:18182) simply advances PC. An external arbiter may act on the prematurely released lock; actual corruption by a second master was not measured in this probe.

Separate an architectural abandonment of a locked sequence from routine pipeline flushing. This is independent of finding 1: the two defects can mask one another by releasing the lock before fetch starvation occurs.

### 3. P2 — CLA overlapping the first TA/TBI corrupts the next longword transfer

When the first line beat is burst inhibited, the [TBI path](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_biu.sv:586) sets `a32` to the next logical longword. The later [CLA block](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_biu.sv:616) assigns the same register from the CLA address counter and overrides that continuation value. If CLA already advanced the address before TA and is asserted again with TA/TBI, the next independent transfer skips a longword. [S_GAP](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_biu.sv:493) also selects write data using this incorrect address.

Example: start a line at `$1000`, assert CLA on the clock before the first acknowledgement, then assert CLA together with the first TA/TBI. The next TS presents `$1008`, although the second longword must be `$1004`. Reads return the value addressed at `$1008` as line slot 1; writes select and address the wrong longword.

The [independent BIU probe](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/tb_bus_transfers.sv) crosses reads/writes, all four starting offsets, full/half/quarter BCLK, and two CLA sampling choices. **24 of 96 cases fail**, each on both continuation address and transferred value. The 72 controls pass when CLA has no early advance or is negated on the TA/TBI edge. See the [matrix log](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/cla-tbi.log).

MC68060UM §7.7.7, p. 7-32, explicitly permits CLA before TA and samples it independently of acknowledgement/ignore states. Sections 7.7.2 and 7.7.4 require TBI to finish the remaining three longwords of the original line. Keep the CLA-visible burst address separate from the logical continuation, or give TBI's continuation update priority. The ordinary slave regression asserts CLA with beat acknowledgements, so it misses the early-CLA interaction.

### 4. P2 — RESET asserts RSTO before an outstanding instruction transfer finishes

The [RESET request](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_backend.sv:2005) waits for `sb_empty`, but that condition only describes the store buffer. It does not wait for the BIU's current instruction transfer. [Masking fetch requests](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060.sv:173) prevents new grants while RESET is pending but does not drain a previously granted transfer. The [RSTO generator](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_biu.sv:372) asserts immediately upon the request.

In the [cached-code reproducer](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/reset_cached_0.s), RSTO is already asserted at cycle 572 while the BIU remains in S_DATA for the `$2010` instruction line. Further reads complete at cycles 575 and 580 with RSTO still asserted. See the [full-clock trace](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/reset_cached_0-full.log). The [half-clock trace](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/reset_cached_0-half.log) likewise shows three completions during RSTO.

All 16 cached-code cases fail across eight RESET instruction positions and two bus configurations. Eight of 16 uncached-code cases also fail, depending on transfer overlap. Other positions pass. The programs finish because the simulated memory continues answering during RSTO; a peripheral being reset need not do so.

MC68060UM §2.8.4, p. 2-12, requires all processor bus cycles to be suspended before RSTO asserts. Drain outstanding transfers before starting the 512-CLK pulse, while retaining arbitration and snooping. The existing [RSTO checker](/home/adam/AP68060/tb/tb_ap68060.sv:348) only detects a new TS during RSTO, so the earlier fix and regression do not cover an already active transfer. The claim in [STATUS.md](/home/adam/AP68060/doc/STATUS.md:40) that RSTO starts after every bus cycle is done remains too strong.

### 5. P2 — Interrupt acknowledge and LPSTOP drive CIOUT asserted

The [acknowledge translation override](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_dmu.sv:669) correctly bypasses translation/caching by setting internal cache mode `10`. However, [read transfer setup](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_dmu.sv:1535) and [write transfer setup](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_dmu.sv:1666) copy that mode's inhibit bit to `bo.ci`, and [bus-request construction](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060_dmu.sv:1069) forwards it unchanged for TT=3 cycles.

MC68060UM Table 7-2, p. 7-9, explicitly specifies **CIOUT negated** for interrupt acknowledge, LPSTOP broadcast, and breakpoint acknowledge. The probe observes `ciout_n=0` on interrupt reads at `$FFFFFFFF` and LPSTOP word writes at `$FFFFFFFE`. UPA, LOCK, and LOCKE satisfy the same checks.

Instrumenting the existing `t_pins`, `t_irq7`, and `t_halt` programs reproduces **18 incorrect CIOUT assertions in 18 acknowledge cycles**, across full BCLK and half BCLK with waits. All six programs otherwise finish with PASS. Evidence: [interrupts](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/ack-t_irq7-full.log), [LPSTOP and interrupt wakeup](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/ack-t_halt-full.log), [half-clock LPSTOP](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/ack-t_halt-half.log).

Keep internal cache bypass and external CIOUT encoding separate for acknowledge cycles. Breakpoint acknowledge shares the setup path but was not dynamically exercised; the counted observations concern IACK and LPSTOP only. This is a confirmed pin-encoding violation; a downstream decoder failure was not demonstrated.

### 6. P2 — The required 27-CLK reset recovery delay is absent

The [top-level reset synchronizer](/home/adam/AP68060/rtl/ap68060.sv:88) releases the core after two enabled bus clocks, without the additional recovery interval specified by MC68060UM §7.13, pp. 7-71–7-73 and Figure 7-48.

With RSTI asserted for 128 CLK cycles before release—longer than the required ten BCLK at either tested ratio—the [full-clock probe](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/reset-gap-full.log) observes the first driven TS by CLK 6 after release. The [half-clock probe](/home/adam/AP68060/obj/audit-bus-2026-10-12/reset-gap-half.log) observes it by CLK 9. Both precede the specified 27-CLK hold period. Reset-mode inputs and BG are stable in these tests.

Add the recovery hold while preserving reset assertion/release sequencing and arbitration. This reconfirms the missing delay already listed in [the original audit](/home/adam/AP68060/doc/AUDIT.md:89); it is not a newly introduced regression. The separate reset-tenure and pin-release tests still pass.
